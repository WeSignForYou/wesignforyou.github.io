What is an apostille?

An apostille is a certificate that authenticates the signature of a public official on a document, so it can be used in another country. Specifically, it certifies three things:

  • that the signature of the public official who signed the document is authentic
  • the capacity in which that public official acted
  • where appropriate, the identity of the seal or stamp the document bears — a notary public seal, for example

An apostille does not validate the contents of your document. It speaks only to the signature, the signer's authority, and the seal. If the information inside the document is wrong, an apostille will not fix it.

The Secretary of State issues a single apostille for any destination country. The document itself has to qualify first: it must be an original notarized document, or a certified record signed by a California public official. Photocopies are never accepted.

How it works

  1. Tell us what you need

    Share the document and the country it is going to. We confirm whether it qualifies and what the Secretary of State will require, before you spend anything.

  2. Get the document into acceptable form

    Some documents need to be notarized before they can be apostilled, and we can do that in person at your location. Others, such as vital records and court documents, are apostilled as issued and need a certified copy from the agency that holds them. We'll tell you which applies to yours.

  3. We ship it to the Secretary of State

    We prepare the apostille request and send your original documents to the California Secretary of State in Sacramento by overnight shipping, with an overnight return label included so they come straight back to us. Overnight both ways is the most we can do to speed things along — the processing time in between is the state's.

  4. You get your documents back

    Processing at the Secretary of State generally takes around 10 to 12 working days, depending on their current queue. As soon as your apostilled originals reach us, we get them back to you.

Plan ahead — apostille is not a same-day service

Our 24/7 availability covers notarization. An apostille is different: your original documents have to be physically shipped to the California Secretary of State in Sacramento, and the processing time there is theirs to set — generally around 10 to 12 working days. We use overnight shipping in both directions to keep the parts we control as fast as possible, but we can't speed up the state's queue. Tell us your deadline when you get in touch and we'll tell you honestly whether it's achievable.

What it costs

We charge one flat fee per document, quoted upfront before any work begins. It rolls together everything getting the apostille takes:

  • the California Secretary of State's processing fee per apostille
  • overnight shipping both ways — the label to send your documents to Sacramento and the overnight return label to bring them back — which we arrange and handle
  • preparing your apostille request and tracking it from start to finish

Get in touch with your document and the destination country, and we'll give you the exact figure — no surprises, no hidden add-ons.

The state processing fee is set by the California Secretary of State and is subject to change.
